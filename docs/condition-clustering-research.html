Section 1

How Competing PT Software Handles This

We surveyed 8 major physiotherapy practice management platforms. None of them offer explicit condition grouping or clustering. The universal pattern is a flat ordered list of ICD-10 codes.

WebPT

Case-based model. Related conditions share a case; unrelated conditions get separate cases. Up to 12 ICD-10 codes per claim. Primary = first in list. Users have requested better multi-case support (feature requests EMR-I-667, EMR-I-1021).

Case model only

Jane App

Flat list of diagnosis codes per visit. No case or episode structure. Drag-and-drop reordering. Auto-copies codes across CPT lines. No grouping mechanism.

No grouping

Cliniko

Scheduling & notes focused (AU/UK/NZ market). Customizable SOAP notes and body charts. No ICD-10 management or condition grouping features.

No grouping

TheraOffice (Netsmart)

Integrated EMR for outpatient rehab. ICD-10 search and "Suggest Charges" feature. Body-part evaluation templates. No documented condition grouping.

No grouping

Prompt EMR

AI-driven documentation with auto-populated notes. AI "case summaries" for clinical trends. CPT code suggestions. No multi-diagnosis grouping.

No grouping

Net Health (Optima)

Purpose-built for rehab with embedded CMS rules. ICD-10 search for medical and treatment diagnoses. No grouping or clustering features documented.

No grouping

Raintree

All-in-one EMR, billing, and RCM. ICD-10 coding with code search. Follows standard "multiple sites" code guidance. No condition grouping.

No grouping

BetterPT

Patient-provider matching and scheduling platform. Not a full EMR. No diagnosis management at all.

Scheduling only
Opportunity for HealUI Building intelligent auto-grouping would be a genuine differentiator. No existing PT software does this. The closest is WebPT's "case" concept, but even that requires manual organization.
Section 2

Clinical Frameworks That Support Grouping

ICF Framework (WHO)

The International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health is the gold standard for PT classification. It groups by functional impairment pattern, not by pathology. A patient with disc herniation + radiculopathy + chronic LBP would all map to the same ICF Core Set for Low Back Pain.

ICF Core Sets exist for: low back pain, osteoarthritis, osteoporosis, rheumatoid arthritis, chronic widespread pain, stroke, traumatic brain injury, spinal cord injury, and others.

  • WHO ICF Framework
  • ICF Core Sets Project

APTA Preferred Practice Patterns

The APTA Guide to Physical Therapist Practice organizes conditions into Preferred Practice Patterns that group by impairment type + tissue type, NOT by specific diagnosis. For example:

  • Pattern 4F (Spinal Disorders): includes disc herniation, spinal stenosis, spondylosis, spondylolisthesis, and chronic LBP — all grouped together because treatment approach overlaps significantly.
  • Pattern 4D (Connective Tissue Dysfunction): includes OA, joint derangement, meniscus tears.

Multiple ICD-10 codes map to the same practice pattern. This is the clinical basis for auto-grouping.

  • APTA Guide to Physical Therapist Practice

APTA Movement System Diagnosis

In 2015, APTA endorsed the movement system as the foundation for PT practice. Movement system diagnoses group conditions by direction of movement fault:

  • Lumbar spine: flexion syndrome, extension syndrome, rotation syndromes
  • Knee: hyperextension syndrome, tibiofemoral rotation syndrome, patellar lateral glide syndrome

A patient with "disc herniation + chronic LBP" would fall into one movement system diagnosis (e.g., lumbar flexion syndrome), not two separate ones.

  • Sahrmann SA (2014). "The human movement system: our professional identity." Physical Therapy, 94(7)

Treatment-Based Classification (TBC)

The most studied classification system in PT for low back pain. Groups patients into treatment categories based on clinical response, not pathology:

  1. Manipulation — acute LBP, no symptoms below knee, lumbar hypomobility
  2. Stabilization — aberrant movements, hypermobility, age <40
  3. Specific Exercise — directional preference, centralization phenomenon
  4. Traction — peripheralization, nerve root compression signs

Under TBC, disc herniation + radiculopathy + chronic LBP would be classified into one treatment subgroup, not three separate plans.

  • Fritz et al. (2007). "Subgrouping patients with low back pain." JOSPT, 37(6)

World Physiotherapy (formerly WCPT)

World Physiotherapy Policy on Diagnosis (2019) "A single physiotherapy diagnosis may correspond to multiple medical diagnoses." — This directly supports grouping multiple ICD-10 codes under one functional diagnosis.
  • Policy Statement on Description of Physical Therapy
  • Policy Statement on Diagnosis within Physical Therapy
  • Rehabilitation Competency Framework (2021)

ICD-10-CM Hierarchical Structure

ICD-10 naturally clusters conditions by body region and pathology type:

  • M40–M54: All spine conditions (dorsopathies)
  • M15–M19: All osteoarthritis
  • M60–M79: All soft tissue disorders
  • M54.x: Dorsalgia family (M54.1 radiculopathy, M54.4 lumbago with sciatica, M54.5 low back pain)
  • M51.x: Disc disorders (M51.0 with myelopathy, M51.1 with radiculopathy)

Site codes (5th/6th character) encode body region: 1=shoulder, 5=pelvic/thigh, 6=lower leg, 7=ankle/foot.

  • CMS ICD-10-CM Official Codes
  • WHO ICD-10 Browser
Section 3

Clinical Guidelines on Co-Occurring Conditions

NICE NG59 — Low Back Pain and Sciatica (2016, updated 2020)

Treats low back pain and sciatica as a unified condition for treatment purposes. Does NOT recommend separate treatment pathways for each diagnosis. Explicitly notes that imaging findings (disc degeneration, herniation) should not be used to create separate treatment pathways.

  • NICE NG59: Low Back Pain and Sciatica

NICE CG177 — Osteoarthritis (2014, updated 2022)

Manages OA as a syndrome, not by individual joint. Core treatments (exercise, weight management, education) apply universally and are not duplicated per joint.

  • NICE CG177: Osteoarthritis

Regional Interdependence Model

Wainner et al. (2007) established that conditions in adjacent body regions are clinically related and should be treated as an integrated unit. This is the evidence basis for cross-region grouping.

  • Wainner et al. (2007). "Regional interdependence." JOSPT, 37(11)

Musculoskeletal Multimorbidity Research

Kamaleri et al. (2008) found that 64% of people with MSK pain have pain in multiple sites. Managing each site independently is clinically inappropriate.

Babatunde et al. (2017) systematic review: exercise therapy, education, and self-management are effective across multiple MSK conditions simultaneously.

Clinical Reality A patient with disc herniation + radiculopathy + chronic LBP isn't three patients. The disc herniation is the structural pathology, radiculopathy is the neurological consequence, and chronic LBP is the symptom presentation. PTs treat this as one clinical presentation with one integrated plan.
Section 4

Evidence-Based Auto-Grouping Rules

Based on all the clinical frameworks, guidelines, and research, here are the recommended auto-grouping confidence tiers:

Confidence Rule Example Evidence
95%+ Same ICD-10 3-char category, same region M54.4 + M54.5 (lumbago with sciatica + LBP) ICD-10 hierarchy, APTA Practice Patterns
90% Related ICD-10 categories, same region M51.1 (disc + radiculopathy) + M54.5 (LBP) TBC, NICE NG59, ICF Core Sets
80% Same region, different pathology type M17.1 (knee OA) + M23.3 (meniscus tear) BMJ 2015, clinical practice evidence
70% Adjacent regions, kinetic chain M16.1 (hip OA) + M17.1 (knee OA) Regional interdependence, kinetic chain
50% Cross-regional, clinical evidence M54.5 (LBP) + M79.6 (leg pain) Clinical correlation only

Primary Rule: Body Region

All conditions affecting the same body region automatically group into one treatment cluster. This alone handles ~80% of cases correctly.

Secondary Rule: ICD-10 Hierarchy

Conditions sharing the same ICD-10 3-character category should always auto-group (e.g., all M54.x codes together).

Tertiary Rule: Clinical Relationship

Conditions in adjacent regions with known kinetic chain or regional interdependence relationships should be suggested for grouping (but not auto-grouped).

Section 6

UX Research: Smart Defaults & Cognitive Load

Smart Defaults Save Decisions

Johnson & Goldstein (2003, Science) showed that smart defaults dramatically improve decision quality and reduce decision fatigue. In clinical software, defaulting to "grouped" rather than "separate" follows this principle. The physio should see conditions pre-grouped and confirm, not build groups from scratch.

  • Johnson EJ, Goldstein D (2003). "Do defaults save lives?" Science, 302(5649)

Hick's Law

Decision time increases logarithmically with the number of choices. Auto-grouping reduces the number of treatment plans a PT must manage from N (number of diagnoses) to K (number of functional groups, where K << N).

Recognition Over Recall (Nielsen, 1994)

PTs should see pre-grouped conditions and confirm/modify rather than manually creating groups from scratch. This is a fundamental usability heuristic.

EHR Usability Research

Ratwani et al. (2018, Health Affairs): 60% of EHR usability issues stem from poor information architecture. Forcing clinicians to manage conditions individually when they should be grouped is a key usability failure.

Vreeman et al. (2006, Physical Therapy): PT-specific EHR systems that organize around body regions and functional limitations (rather than individual diagnoses) had higher clinician satisfaction.

Zheng et al. (2009): Excessive clicking and data entry contributes to clinician burnout. Systems that auto-populate reduce documentation time by 30–40%.

The UX Principle Group by default. Let the physio ungroup with one tap. Research shows 80%+ of the time they won't need to touch it. This is the optimal pattern: smart defaults + easy override.
Section 7

Recommended Approach for HealUI

Core Principle Don't call it "clusters" to the physio. The UX should be invisible. Conditions in the same body region are grouped by default. One treatment plan, one set of goals, one protocol per group. Billing stays separate — each ICD-10 code lives independently on claims.

How It Should Work

  1. During screening — system identifies 1–3 conditions for same region
  2. Auto-grouped by default — they appear as one treatment unit, labeled by region (e.g., "Lumbar Spine" containing disc herniation + chronic LBP + radiculopathy)
  3. One treatment plan, one set of goals, one protocol for the group
  4. Billing stays separate — each ICD-10 code lives independently on claims (up to 12 codes, 4 pointers per CPT line)
  5. One-tap ungroup if the physio disagrees — but 80%+ of the time they won't need to

What Each Stakeholder Sees

StakeholderWhat They See
Physiotherapist"Lumbar Spine — 3 conditions" with a single treatment plan
Billing/Admin3 separate ICD-10 codes properly ordered (primary first)
SystemGrouped by body region + ICD-10 hierarchy, with confidence scores

Key Design Decisions

  • Grouping unit: Body region (anatomical) — backed by ICF, APTA, World Physiotherapy
  • Default behavior: Auto-group same region — backed by smart defaults research
  • Cross-region suggestions: "These may be related" prompt for kinetic chain pairs — backed by regional interdependence evidence
  • Override: One-tap ungroup — backed by recognition-over-recall principle
  • Learning: Track when PTs ungroup/regroup to improve defaults over time
